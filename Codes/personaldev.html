The Richest Man in Babylon: Chapter Reviews

Jump to Chapter 5 Review

Chapter 3: 7 Cures for a Lean Purse

This chapter shows Arkad, the richest man in Babylon, teaching a group of citizens in Babylon how to acquire wealth, in response to King Sargon's concern for the majority of his people being unable to properly make money. The Seven Cures, as he refers to it, are as follows:

  1. Start thy purse to fattening
  2. Control thy expenditures
  3. Make thy gold multiply
  4. Guard thy treasures from loss
  5. Make of thy dwelling a profitable investment
  6. Insure a future income
  7. Increase thy ability to earn

These steps each explain the core parts of financial success, which includes saving, budgeting, investing in yourself and guarding against bad investments while looking for good investments, such as owning a home to reduce long-term living costs. These steps resonated with me because as a student with limited resources, this gives me proper insight into how to manage money and know how even now I can use what little I have to slowly build wealth, so that in the future I can build upon the proper foundations and become rich. It is not just about making a lot of money, but about developing the discipline and intelligence to properly manage and invest whatever money I have. If I apply these principles now, I can build a strong financial foundation that I can continue to build upon as I become more financially successful.

Chapter 5: The 5 Laws of Gold

This chapter follows Arkad's son, Nomasir, where Arkad gives him a bag of gold and a tablet with the 5 laws of gold. Initially, Nomasir loses all his gold through reckless spending, getting robbed, and engaging in business with an incompetent partner, eventually losing all his wealth. Then Nomasir decides to read the laws on the tablet, which he now views as more valuable than gold itself. By abiding by these rules, he restores and exceeds his initial wealth. When he returns to his father's home after 10 years, he has three bags of gold, triple what he initially left with. The five laws are as follows:

  1. Gold cometh gladly and in increasing quan tity to any man who will put by not less than one-tenth of his earnings to create an estate for his future and that or his family.
  2. Gold laboureth diligently and contentedly for the wise owner who finds for it profitable employ ment, multiplying even as the flocks of the field.
  3. Gold clingeth to the protection of the cau tious owner who invests it under the advice of men wise in its handling.
  4. Gold slippeth away from the man who in vests it in businesses or purposes with which he is not familiar or which are not approved by those skilled in its keep.
  5. Gold flees the man who would force it to impossible earnings or who followeth the allur ing advice of tricksters and schemers or who trusts it to his own inexperience and romantic desires in investment.

The first law refers to saving one-tenth of everything that is earned, so as not to completely run out of resources. The second law refers to putting money into profitable employment, and not just sitting unused. The third law refers to seeking wise advisors; those who are already well-versed in the knowledge of handling money, as their guidance can only help. The fourth law refers to staying away from investment opportunities that you do not fully understand, as it is a recipe for disaster. The fifth law refers to being aware of and avoiding unrealistic promises of wealth from wicked, manipulative people, as if these are to be believed your wealth can disappear quickly.

These laws show me the fundamental ways to ensure that the money I already have built up does not get squandered. Saving, becoming more financially educated to know how my money can go, seeking proper advice, avoiding opportunities I don't quite understand and staying away from false "get rich quick" schemes can all help me exponentially build on top of already earned wealth.

Through my readings, I came to realise that both chapters are stacking building blocks onto the same foundation. Chapter 3 gives the step-by-step guide into how to begin building wealth, while Chapter 5 gives the rules on how to grow the wealth I have already accumulated. If I use both properly and remain committed to the process, I believe I can apply these lessons to build wealth and achieve greater financial stability in the future.

My biggest takeaway from this book is Nomasir's riches to rags to riches story. His experience in the world of youthful recklessness and foolish decisions led him to hit rock bottom, but he did not give up. He instead recognized he had made a mistake by not going through the rules he had been given. He did not resign himself to "bad luck" or a victim of unfortunate events, but rather he began building again, this time the right way. This for me means that no matter what happens, no matter how bad life gets, I should just continue to build because there is always light at the end of the tunnel once I do what is right and stay committed.
